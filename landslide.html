LANDSLIDES

When slopes become unstable, the earth shifts without warning. Understand landslides, their causes, and how to stay safe in high-risk areas.

Understanding Landslides

What Is A Landslide?

A landslide is when a big chunk of rock, soil or debris moves down a slope. This happens when gravity pulls harder, than the material holding the slope together.

Landslides can be caused by rain, earthquakes, volcanic activity or things people do like changing the shape of a slope or cutting down trees.

Why It Happens

Main Causes Of Landslides

Recognizing slope stability factors can help prevent disasters.

Heavy Rainfall

When there is much rain the soil becomes saturated with water. The water makes the soil heavier. Because of that the soil loses its cohesion. As a result mudslides and soil collapse happen.

Seismic Activity

During earthquake tremors the slopes become unstable. Unstable slopes can cause rock falls and large‑scale landslides.

Human Activities

Deforestation removes the roots that hold soil particles together. Without these roots slopes become weak. Improper digging of slopes and a poor drainage system hurt hills.

The Consequences

Effects Of Landslides

Landslides pose immediate risks to infrastructure, environments, and human lives.

🏡

Property Destruction

Structures built on or below steep slopes can be buried or structurally destroyed.

🚧

Blocked Infrastructure

Debris can block major highways, railways, and rivers, causing secondary flooding.

🌲

Habitat Loss

Entire forest acres, wildlife, and natural ecosystems can be swept away rapidly.

⚡

Utility Outages

Power, water, and communication networks can suffer severe disruptions.

Be Prepared

How Can We Reduce Landslide Risks?

Proper land management and slope stabilization save lives.

01. Plant Deep-Rooted Vegetation

The trees, shrubs, and ground covers will contribute to slope stability because the roots of the plants bind the soil together. The vegetation will decrease soil erosion since the runoff on the land will slow down, and the soil will be protected against heavy rains. It is important to protect forests and plant vegetation to keep the slope stable, especially for hilly and mountainous regions.

02. Construct Retaining Walls

The retaining walls may help to support the unstable slope because they hold the soil, preventing its displacement. They have to be constructed in accordance with the slope's specifications and be accompanied by drainage systems, as the water pressure behind the wall should not be increased.

03. Proper Slope Drainage

Put in place drainage trenches, pipes, and ditches to ensure that there is proper flow of excess water from the area. Proper drainage will make sure that there is no buildup of water in the soil. This buildup of water may cause soil to get heavier, making the slopes unstable.

04. Avoid Risky Construction

It is important to stay away from constructing buildings and other forms of infrastructure on steep, unstable slopes. The construction of infrastructure may involve digging in the area, clearing vegetation, and cutting down slopes. There should be landslide hazard maps to ensure that there is proper evaluation of the areas that are to be developed.

Stay Safe

Landslide Safety Guide

Know what action to take before, during, and after slope instability events.

Before A Landslide

  • Monitor local weather and heavy rainfall warnings.
  • Learn the history of landslides in your area.
  • Watch for warning signs like cracking soil or tilting trees.
  • Prepare an emergency kit and evacuation route.

During A Landslide

  • Evacuate immediately if instructed or safe to do so.
  • Listen for unusual sounds like cracking trees or roaring debris.
  • If trapped, curl into a tight ball and protect your head.
  • Stay alert while driving near steep embankments.

After A Landslide

  • Stay clear of the slide zone; secondary slides may occur.
  • Check for trapped or injured persons near the edges.
  • Report damaged power and water lines to authorities.
  • Listen to local radio or officials for safety guidance.

Awareness Today. Safety Tomorrow.

Understanding slope stability and staying cautious during intense weather can save lives and protect communities.
